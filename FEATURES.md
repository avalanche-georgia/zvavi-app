# Features

## Opening the Site

Opening the site takes visitors straight to the home page of the region they chose before. Visitors who haven't chosen one yet see the region chooser — or, while there is only one region, go directly to it. Following a link to another region's page doesn't change the visitor's chosen region.

## Region Chooser

Shows today's avalanche danger for every forecast region at a glance: a compact list, one row per region with its danger level — or a clear note when the forecast has expired, no forecast was issued today, it's off-season, or the region doesn't have a forecaster yet — next to a map of every forecast area coloured by its danger level with a numbered marker, which zooms in and out but always stays around Georgia. Hovering a region in the list highlights it on the map and the other way round. Choosing a region opens its home page and remembers it for the next visit. Between June and November, when no forecasts are being issued, the page says so. Below are the main partners — or an invitation to become one — and short introductions to the organisation and how to help, including donating.

## Site Header and Footer

Every public page shares the same header and footer. The header has a menu named after the region you're looking at, with that region's pages (overview, current forecast, forecast area, past forecasts, observations), a "Switch region" button when there is more than one region, plus weather stations, partners and the about pages, and an EN / ქარ language switch. Visitors don't need an account, so there is no sign-in link; team members sign in through the admin address, after which the header shows their account menu (admin panel, profile, sign out). The logo always leads back to the region's home page. On phones and tablets these move into a menu. The footer repeats the region's pages, lists resources (starting with "All regions" when there is a choice) and organisation pages, the team's social channels, the emergency number 112 and the legal pages.

## Region Page

Each region has its own home page. It opens with the current overall danger level on the official 1–5 scale, when the forecast was issued, how long it stays valid and who wrote it — the forecaster's name opens their team profile when they're on the public Team page. Tapping the danger level explains what it means; "Read full forecast" opens the forecast. If the latest forecast's validity has run out and no new one has been issued yet, a notice says so. A region that has never had a forecast says that we're looking for a forecaster, with a link to join. Below are the region's main partners (or an invitation to become one), the three most recent avalanche reports — each opens in place with its details — with shortcuts to see all of them or report a new one, and quick links to the forecast area, observations, the weather stations and past forecasts.

## Avalanche Forecasts

Daily professional forecasts for each active region in the Georgian backcountry. Each forecast includes:

- **Danger levels** for multiple elevation zones (sub-alpine, alpine, high-alpine) on the standard 1–5 European scale
- **Avalanche problem breakdown** — up to 9 problem types (storm slab, wind slab, persistent slab, wet slab, cornices, etc.) with affected aspects, elevation bands, likelihood, and size
- **Narratives** — weather outlook, snowpack assessment, and additional hazard notes written by certified forecasters
- **Recent avalanche history** — observed natural and human-triggered events with location, size, and date, taken from the region's avalanche catalog. Only reviewed, published records appear on a public forecast

The forecast page is built for phones first: the overall danger level sits at the top with an explanation one tap away, followed by danger by elevation, the summary, the avalanche problems, recent avalanches, and snowpack and weather. A row of shortcuts jumps between sections. Each avalanche problem opens a panel showing where it is expected (by aspect and elevation), its size, sensitivity, distribution, trend and time of day — each with a short explanation of its scale. The forecaster's name opens their team profile when they're on the public Team page. Arrows step to the previous or next forecast, and an older forecast is clearly marked with a link back to the current one.

Forecasts are authored in a draft/publish workflow, so forecasters can prepare and review before going live.

## Forecast Area Map

Interactive map showing the forecast area boundary for each region, giving users a clear spatial reference for where the forecast applies.

## Forecast Archive

Full history of past forecasts, browsable by date, so users can track how conditions evolved through the season.

## Weather Stations

Links to real-time data from mountain weather stations in the forecast area.

## Member Verification

Each team member receives a unique QR code. Scanning it opens a verification page that confirms their current membership status, helping partners quickly authenticate credentials.

## Join / Apply

A public application form for individuals interested in joining the avalanche forecasting team. Submitted applications are reviewed by administrators.

## Public Observations

Anyone can report an avalanche they've witnessed — no account needed. The submission form is built for phones in the field: large, easy-to-tap choices instead of dropdowns, one-tap dates (today, yesterday, a picked date or "not sure"), and a bar that stays on screen showing which required answers are still missing. It asks the person to mark the location on a map (required, so every new report can be shown on a map) — by tapping, dragging the pin, using their phone's current location, or typing coordinates — optionally name the place, choose the avalanche type, trigger and size (each size explained in plain words), pick the affected slopes on a compass for each elevation band (tap or drag across directions, and copy a selection to the other bands in one tap), describe what they saw, attach up to three photos, and optionally share their name and background. If they choose "Remember my details on this device", their details are kept in that browser only and the next report starts with a short "Reporting as" summary instead of empty fields; "Forget me" removes them at any time. The Privacy Policy explains what is collected with a report, what is published and how remembered details are handled. Photos are shrunk automatically on the reporter's phone before sending, so even large camera shots upload quickly on a mountain connection, and each one can be previewed full-screen before submitting. Photos are stored without any hidden metadata such as the location where they were taken. After sending, a confirmation screen offers to submit another report or go to the observations page. Every submission is reviewed by the forecasting team before it appears publicly, so nothing unchecked reaches the site. Until then, the person who sent it sees their own report on the observations page marked "Under review" (on that device only), and the team gets a notification with a direct link to review it.

The observations page shows every published report — those sent in by the public and those logged by the forecasting team — as a list and on a map of the region side by side (on phones, switch between the two). The list loads more reports as you scroll, and the map always shows every matching report, grouping nearby ones into numbered bubbles that split apart as you zoom in. Reports are grouped by date — today, yesterday, this week, then by month — and can be filtered to the last 7 or 30 days, the current season (November to May) or a custom date range, based either on when the avalanche happened or when it was reported — reports whose date the observer didn't know have their own filter — and sorted newest or largest first. Each report shows its place name when one was given, and opens into a detail view with its photos (tap for full-screen), size, trigger, dimensions, the slopes affected, the description and the reporter's first name with last initial. Reports logged by the team carry a "Team" badge, with the name of the team member who logged them when known. From there, "Show on map" gives a close-up map with copyable coordinates and a link to open the spot in a maps app. Filtered views and individual reports have their own links that can be shared. Contact details and other personal information submitted with a report are only ever visible to forecasters.

## About, Join and Contact

Short pages about the organisation: who we are and what we do (with a clear note that forecasts are guidance, not a guarantee); how to get involved — becoming a member, volunteering, donating via Patreon, Wise or bank transfer, and spreading avalanche awareness; and how to reach the team, with every bank detail copyable in one tap and a reminder that we are not a rescue service (call 112).

## Team

A public "Our team" page, reachable from the About menu and the footer, introduces the people behind the forecasts in two groups: forecasters and the product team. Each person appears with their photo (or initials), name, their role on the team and a short bio; tapping someone opens their full profile, with links to their social profiles shown with each platform's logo. Nobody is listed by default. On a Team page in the admin panel, the team drags people into a group and into the order the public page shows them, and switches each person on or off (people can also switch themselves on their profile page). Email addresses are never shown. The page ends with an invitation to contribute — observations, code, design, translation or outreach.

## Partners

A public Partners page thanks the businesses and organisations that help pay for the free forecast. Partners appear in three levels, told apart by the size of their card: main partners get a large card, partners a medium one and supporters a small one. Every active partner is always shown — nothing scrolls or hides behind "Show all" — and within each level the order is shuffled on every visit, so no one keeps the top spot. Each card shows the partner's logo and name, a short description (with "Read more" when it is longer) and one link to their website or social page, named after where it goes (e.g. "Instagram" or "ozon.ge"). Perks for forecast users are spelled out on main partner cards and sit behind a small gift icon on the others. On phones, supporters become a grid of logos; tapping one opens its details. When there is no main partner, the top spot invites businesses to become one, and the page ends with an invitation to get in touch about becoming a partner.

## Admin Panel

A password-protected back-office for the forecasting team. The admin area and the team's data are limited to administrators and forecasters; any other account sees a "No access" screen.

### Forecast Authoring
Forecasters can create, edit, duplicate, and publish forecasts through a single-page form with a section index on the side. Hazard levels are set on one-tap scales for the overall danger and each elevation zone, with a live preview of the public forecast graphic and a hint when the overall level is lower than the highest zone. Avalanche problems are shown as cards and edited in a side panel (type, size, likelihood, distribution, timing and the affected slopes, which are required), and cards can be dragged to set priority or removed with an undo. Every forecast needs a summary. The forecaster is filled in with whoever creates the forecast and can be changed to another forecaster, or to another person typed by name; forecasts by a team member are linked to their profile. Recent avalanches are linked from the region's avalanche catalog rather than retyped: forecasters pick existing records (including drafts and reports still under review), create a new record without leaving the form, or edit a linked record — changes to a record show everywhere it is used. The form can be saved at any time and kept open (also with Ctrl/⌘ + S), shows when it was last saved, and asks before discarding unsaved changes. Drafts are invisible to the public until explicitly published.

The forecasts list opens on a card showing the forecast the public site currently displays for the region — its danger level by elevation, when it was published and how long it stays valid — with shortcuts to open the public page or start the next forecast from a copy. If that forecast's validity has run out, the card says so, since the public site keeps showing it until a new one is published. Below it, the list can be filtered by status (published or drafts), by when forecasts were created (last 30 days, the current season or custom dates) and searched by forecast number, forecaster or summary; filtered views can be shared as a link. Each row shows the overall danger with a small per-elevation pyramid, the forecast number and summary, validity with time remaining, and whether it is current, published or a draft. Publishing a draft first shows a short summary to confirm — region, danger levels and how long it stays valid, noting that it goes public on the site and to partner apps at once — and is refused when the forecast has no valid-until date in the future; publishing or unpublishing can be undone right away; editing, duplicating, copying the public link and deleting (with a confirmation) are in each row's menu. On small screens rows turn into compact cards. The home page map, the current-forecast page and every forecast page agree on which forecast is current in each region.

Each forecast has its own read-only page in the admin. It shows the forecast the way it was written — danger by elevation, summary, avalanche problems, linked avalanche records and conditions — with a clear note on whether the public site is showing it right now, whether its validity has run out, or whether a newer forecast has replaced it. Forecasters can edit, duplicate, publish or unpublish (with undo), copy the public link or delete it from there, and open any linked avalanche record without leaving the page. Editing a forecast from its page returns to that view when done.

### Recent Avalanche Catalog
Administrators can browse all recorded avalanches in a compact paginated table showing who submitted each one, and filter it by occurrence or creation date, by source (team or public), by status, and to records no forecast uses yet. Each row shows whether it is attached to a forecast, and to how many. Clicking a record opens it in a side panel next to the list, with everything about it — photos, dimensions, slopes affected, location, description, who submitted it and which forecasts it's attached to — and it can be edited right there without losing your place in the list. Arrows (or the keyboard) step to the previous or next record, and "Show on map" opens a close-up map with copyable coordinates. Records can be published or unpublished with one click; archiving and restoring are done by changing the status while editing, and an unfinished edit is never thrown away without asking. Each record shows whether it was logged by the forecasting team or submitted by the public. New records are added in the same side panel, next to the list. Adding and editing a record uses the same form as the public report — the same map, choices and photo upload — plus team-only fields: a short place name, involvement notes and the status. Administrators can attach up to three photos to any record and remove existing ones, including photos sent in with public reports; removed photos are deleted for good once the edit is saved. A location on the map is required whenever a record is saved.

### Observation Review
Public submissions wait in a dedicated review queue, oldest first, until a forecaster approves them (one click) or rejects them. After each decision the panel moves on to a neighbouring report still waiting, so a queue can be cleared without going back to the list. Approved reports join the public observations page and the avalanche catalog; rejected ones are archived, not deleted. The admin menu shows how many reports are waiting, and each region tab shows its own count. The admin overview shows the waiting count too, with a shortcut to the review queue and the latest submission in its recent activity.

### Member Management
Administrators can manage the team roster: add members — including people who joined before the online application existed, with the same personal details an application asks for (age, gender, location, occupation, motivation) — update their status (active, inactive, pending, suspended, expired), set membership validity dates, and generate or view their verification QR codes. The roster can be searched by name, member ID or email, filtered by status and sorted; each member opens in a side panel next to the list, where they can be viewed, edited or — for a pending application — approved with their membership dates.

### Staff Profiles
Forecasters and administrators can view and edit their own profile — including display name, a short role on the team (shown as their badge on the public Team page), a short bio, a profile photo and up to five links to their social profiles or website. The photo and name are shown in the admin panel header. When creating a forecast, the forecaster field is automatically prefilled with the logged-in user's name.

### Weather Station Management
Administrators can manage the list of mountain weather stations: add, edit, delete, and reorder them via drag-and-drop. Each station has a name in English and Georgian, an altitude, and a link to its live data feed.

### Partner Benefits Management
Administrators can manage a list of partner organizations and the exclusive discounts or perks they offer to Avalanche Georgia members. Each partner has a name, optional benefit description, logo, and website — all stored in both English and Georgian. The list can be searched by name, filtered by active / inactive and sorted by name or tier; each row has an Active switch that shows or hides the partner on the public site at once; a partner is added or edited in a side panel next to the list. Active partners are displayed to the public in a "Member Benefits" panel accessible from the Join Us page and the member verification card.

## Privacy-Friendly Visitor Statistics

Visitors are asked once, alongside the safety disclaimer, whether they allow anonymous usage statistics; the answer is optional and never blocks the site. Visitors who agreed to the disclaimer earlier see a clearly visible notice instead. A "No thanks" is remembered for 6 months, after which the visitor is asked again (never for visitors whose browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal); an "Allow" is never asked again. Anyone can change their choice later from the "Analytics preferences" link in the footer. The statistics show how many people use the site, which pages and regions they visit and where they come from, and — for each partner — how often their logo was shown, clicked, and how many visitors went on to their website. Admin pages are never counted. The Privacy Policy and Terms of Service describe what is collected.
